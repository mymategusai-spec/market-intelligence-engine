> **`YES, BUT NOT AS AN INVESTMENT`** (`DECISIONS.md` D-0019)
>
> A well-chosen property can largely pay for itself. It does not produce a return that
> compensates for the capital at risk.

No property, in any scenario modelled, yields more than **3.17%** on total project cost — before
Japanese income tax. But a 16–22 guest lodge generates **A$13,000–23,000 NOI** in the base case,
after all operating costs *and* after three weeks of owner use at peak rates.

**That is precisely what the brief asked for** — that tourism "materially support ownership costs".
It is not an investment return. Whether that trade is attractive is the owners' call.

## The findings that matter most

**1. Myoko's municipal average was hiding the answer.** Akakura **+7.07%**, Myoko Kogen **+3.54%**,
against the municipality at **−0.79%**. The bear case rested on "capital hasn't moved land prices";
it has, where it was deployed. Myoko upgraded to `YES, IN AKAKURA SPECIFICALLY` (D-0017).

**2. Snow quantity and resilience are inversely related.** JMA 1991–2020: Myoko **1,055 cm DJFM at
0.0 °C**; Furano **535 cm at −8.3 °C**. Myoko gets 97% more snow with 8.3 °C less thermal margin.
Optimising for snow *today* points to Myoko/Nozawa; for snow in *2040*, to Hokkaido. The 10–15 year
horizon sits between them. **This overturned the earlier elevation-inferred scores in both
directions** — Myoko had the most snow and was scored second-lowest; Furano the least and was
scored highest.

**3. Capacity should be maximised, not optimised** (D-0018). Yield rises monotonically —
Hakuba 1.21% at 6 guests to 3.54% at 24. Remote ownership carries ~A$8,450/yr of fixed cost:
**26–44% of revenue at 6 guests, 7–11% at 24.** Small properties cannot carry the cost of being
owned from Australia.

**4. Vendor yield claims are ~3× the modelled figure.** Echo Hotel Hakuba markets "circa 10% net";
models produce max 3.17%. Either the models are materially too conservative — improving the whole
project's economics — or the claims are inflated. **Unresolved, and it moves the central
conclusion either way.**

**5. Owner use costs more than the NOI.** Three weeks at peak ≈ **A$25,000/yr** at a 16-guest
Hakuba property. **Separate owner quarters is the single highest-value property attribute
identified anywhere.**

**6. Winter occupancy answers differ sharply by prefecture.** Niigata resort hotels go 34.0% annual
(rank 45) → **64.9% January (rank 4)**. Nagano reaches only 45.0% (rank 20), and Nagano simple
lodging is **14.2% in January — below its own annual average**. That is a direct caution on Hakuba.

**7. Hakuba's late season is measurably collapsing.** April visits **−38.5%**, May **−82.7%** year
on year, while Dec–Feb held or grew. First directly measured climate signal in the project.
