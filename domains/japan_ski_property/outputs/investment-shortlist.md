## 3. What the shortlist says about the strategy

**1. Operating businesses beat conversions.** Four of the top five hold or are an existing licence.
The licence, compliance cost, occupancy and management unknowns — the four largest in the project
— are all resolved by buying something already trading.

**2. Owner quarters matter more than price.** Three weeks of owner use costs ~A$25,000/yr,
exceeding modelled NOI. ③ and ⑤ eliminate this structurally.

**3. Capacity beats cheapness.** Every rejected-on-economics property failed for the same reason:
too small to carry the ~A$8,450/yr fixed cost of remote ownership.

**4. Submarket beats market.** Akakura +7.07% vs Myoko −0.79%. Tsugaike and Misorano are not the
Hakuba dining core. Ikenotaira is not Akakura.

**5. No property yields more than 3.2% on total project cost** in any scenario. This shortlist is
of **lifestyle assets that can largely pay for themselves**, not investments — until either
audited accounts or a resolved vendor-yield discrepancy says otherwise.

## 4. What would have to be true before any purchase

1. **Availability confirmed** — 43 of 51 candidates are `AVAILABILITY UNVERIFIED`.
2. **Audited accounts** for any operating business — resolves the 3× gap between vendor yield
   claims (8–10%) and modelled yields (1–3%).
3. **Independent building inspection** and a **separate renovation estimate** — two engagements,
   never the selling agent.
4. **Municipal licensing check** — the largest open regulatory risk, unchecked everywhere.
5. **Named local management** with a quoted fee.
6. **Distance to lift verified** — a core filter, still empty on every record.
