## 2. The headline: the snowiest markets are the most fragile

`CALCULATION` — **Myoko gets 97% more snow than Furano with 8.3°C less thermal margin.**

| | Myoko | Furano |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DJFM snowfall | **1,055 cm** | 535 cm |
| January mean temperature | **0.0 °C** | **−8.3 °C** |

**Sekiyama's January mean is exactly 0.0 °C.** Myoko has the most snow of any market measured and
sits precisely at the freezing point. A 1–2 °C warming moves a large share of its winter
precipitation from snow to rain *at village level*.

Furano has the least snow and an 8.3 °C buffer. Even substantial warming leaves its precipitation
falling as snow.

This is the mechanism behind the elevation-dependent decline established earlier from the
literature: the low-elevation Japan Sea markets are **warm-snow regimes with almost no thermal
margin**. Their enormous snowfall totals and their fragility are the same fact.

### The corrections are large and they matter

- **Myoko was scored 5.0 and has the most snow of any market measured.** The elevation-based
  inference was wrong on volume and right on fragility — but it conflated the two into one score
  and got both halves wrong as a result.
- **Nozawa was scored lowest at 4.5** on its 565 m base. It has the **deepest measured snowpack**
  of any station sampled (205 cm normal maximum).
- **Furano was scored highest at 8.5** and has the **least snow of any station sampled**. Its
  reputation rests on snow *dryness*, not volume — and that reputation is deserved, but it is a
  quality-of-snow claim, not a quantity claim.
- **Hakuba is roughly confirmed at ~6.0, but for entirely different reasons than assumed.** Its
  630 cm DJFM is second-lowest of the Honshu stations and only 60% of Myoko's. Its real advantages
  are its **1,128 m vertical above the village** — the largest of any candidate, so terrain stays
  skiable as the snowline rises — and a −2.8 °C margin better than Myoko or Nozawa.

## 4. The caveat that must travel with these numbers

**These are valley stations, not mountain stations.** Ski terrain sits roughly 1,000 m above most
of them.

| Market | Station | Summit | Vertical above station |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hakuba | ~703 m | 1,831 m | **+1,128 m** |
| Nozawa | 576 m | 1,650 m | +1,074 m |
| Myoko | ~350 m | ~1,500 m | +1,150 m |

At a standard lapse rate, Hakuba's summit runs roughly 6–7 °C colder than its village. So
**on-mountain conditions are materially better than these figures suggest for every market**, and
Hakuba's large vertical partly offsets its lower valley snowfall.

But the village figures are not merely a proxy — **they describe where the property is**. Village
snow determines road access, whether ski-in/ski-out actually works, how the place looks to guests,
and snow-clearing cost. A market whose village turns to rain while its summit stays white has a
real operating problem even if the skiing is fine.

## 5. What this does to the investment question

**There is a genuine trade-off here, not a ranking.**

- **Optimising for snow today** → Myoko or Nozawa, by a wide margin.
- **Optimising for snow in 2040** → Hokkaido, particularly Furano and Rusutsu.
- **The brief's 10–15 year horizon sits awkwardly between the two.**

This also **partially rehabilitates Myoko**. Its snow was scored as a weakness; measured data makes
it the strongest snow market examined, on volume. What was really being measured was *fragility*,
and that is now scored separately and honestly at 3.0.

Two independent lines now point the same way for Myoko: **excellent product today, structurally
exposed over the hold period.** That is the same shape as the land-price finding — strong as an
operating business, weak as a long-hold appreciation play.

## 6. Gaps

- **No trend analysis.** These are 1991–2020 normals, a single 30-year window. Comparing them with
  1961–1990 normals would measure decline directly per station. **Highest-value next step.**
- Niseko's own station returned a 2010–2020 period with no snow columns; Kutchan is a surface
  station with a different layout. **Neither Niseko nor Kutchan is directly measured here** —
  Kiroro and Rusutsu are used as Hokkaido proxies.
- No Yuzawa, Appi or Shiga Kogen station.
- No mountain-station data for any market.
- Season length not derived; possible from monthly snow-depth normals and not yet done.
