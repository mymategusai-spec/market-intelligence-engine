# Myoko micro-markets — the municipal average was hiding the answer

**Phase:** 7 / D-0015 trigger test · **Status:** The decisive test has been run
**Last updated:** 2026-08-20

> The test defined in `DECISIONS.md` D-0015: *do Akakura-specific land prices rise while the
> municipal average falls?* It was named "the single highest-value outstanding test in the whole
> Myoko question."
>
> **The answer is yes.**

---

## 1. The finding

`FACT` — 2026 published land prices (公示地価):

| Area | ¥/m² | **Change** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Myoko municipality (all points)** | 19,904 | **−0.79%** |
| **Myoko Kogen area (ski submarket)** | **15,200** | **+3.54%** |
| **赤倉 Akakura, Minami 469-14** *(commercial, hot-spring resort)* | **19,700** | **+7.07%** |
| 田口 Taguchi, Minami 288-2 *(commercial/residential, 100m from station)* | 14,600 | −0.68% |
| 田口 Taguchi, Nishihara 888-4 *(residential, 800m from station)* | 10,700 | 0.00% |

2025 prefectural survey for Myoko Kogen: ¥7,883/m², **+4.88%**.

**Akakura is rising at +7.07% while the municipality it sits in is falling at −0.79%.**

The ski submarket is rising on both official series. The station-area and residential points are
flat to slightly negative. **The municipal average is dominated by the Arai-side town and rural
parcels, and it has been masking a genuinely appreciating ski micro-market.**

---

## 2. What this does to the Myoko verdict

`DECISIONS.md` D-0015 recorded Myoko as `WATCH` with an explicit upgrade trigger:

> **Trigger 1 (most important):** Akakura-specific land prices rise while the municipal average
> stays flat.

**That trigger has fired.**

The bear case rested substantially on one argument: *eight years of committed capital have not
moved land prices.* That argument does not survive disaggregation. Capital **has** moved prices —
in the 5,000 m of the municipality where it was actually deployed.

### What changes

| Bear argument | Status after this test |
| --- | --- |
| "Eight years of investment and land hasn't moved" | **Substantially weakened.** Akakura +7.07%, Myoko Kogen +3.54% |
| "Myoko is only ~23% of Niigata's foreign stays" | Unchanged — still true |
| "Population −46.7% by 2050" | Unchanged — still true |
| "Niigata simple-lodging occupancy 11.5%" | **Weakened** — Niigata resort hotels hit 64.9% in January, rank 4 |
| "Snow is fragile" | **Refined** — most snow of any market measured, but 0.0 °C January mean |
| "Land-appreciation case unsupported" | **Overturned at sub-market level** |

### What does not change

**The 14× within-municipality spread is now measured, not inferred.** Akakura at ¥19,700/m²
against rural Myoko parcels at ¥2,550/m². Buying "in Myoko" means nothing; buying **in Akakura**
means something specific.

And the cheap portal stock this project collected — ¥4.8m and ¥8.99m houses in Gakko-cho and
Shinonome-cho — sits in the **flat-to-falling** part of the municipality, not the appreciating
part. **The cheapest listings are in the wrong submarket**, which is precisely the trap
`DECISIONS.md` D-0012 was written to prevent.

## 3. Long-run context, which tempers the optimism

`FACT` — Myoko Kogen peaked at **¥71,000/m² in 1993** and is **¥15,200/m² in 2026** — **−79%**,
with recovery beginning only in 2024.

So Akakura's +7.07% is the second year of a recovery from an eight-decade-low base, not a
sustained trend. Two years of gains after thirty years of decline is a turn, not yet a trajectory.

Compare Hakuba: bottomed 2018, now in its eighth year of recovery, +26.93% in 2026. **Myoko Kogen
in 2026 looks roughly like Hakuba in 2019–2020** — which is the "early-stage" claim the bull case
always made, and which the municipal data appeared to refute but the sub-market data supports.

## 5. Recommended posture change

`HYPOTHESIS`, medium-high confidence:

> **Myoko should move from `WATCH` to a qualified `YES, IN A SPECIFIC SUBMARKET`.**

The qualification is doing real work. The evidence supports:

- **Akakura Onsen specifically** — appreciating, walkable, best snow measured, best guest-experience
  structure, lowest forward supply pressure, and lodge stock actually available.

It does **not** support:

- Myoko municipality generally, whose average is falling.
- The Arai-side town centre, where the cheap listings are.
- Suginosawa on masterplan speculation, until the PCG programme is confirmed under construction.

**Formally recording this requires a new decision entry**, since D-0015's own trigger has fired.

## 6. Remaining tests

1. **Point-level history for the Akakura point** — is +7.07% the second year of gains or a spike?
2. **Ikenotaira and Suginosawa points** — not separately surveyed in the data retrieved.
3. **Transaction prices, not valuations** — MLIT transaction data would show whether buyers are
   actually paying these levels in Akakura. This is the strongest remaining argument for the API
   decision.
4. Whether the Akakura point being **commercial** (hot-spring resort zoning) rather than
   residential explains part of the gap — commercial land can move differently.
