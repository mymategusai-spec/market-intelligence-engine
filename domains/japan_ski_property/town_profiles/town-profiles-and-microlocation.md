## 1. The finding that reorders the micro-location question

`OPINION` (multiple independent sources) — **Hakuba's free shuttle stops running around 6pm.**
Guests staying in one village and dining in another pay for taxis after that.

This is not a detail. It converts Hakuba from *one market* into **several weakly-connected
sub-markets after dark**, and it means a property's village is a bigger determinant of guest
experience than its distance to a lift.

| Destination | Structure | Evening mobility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nozawa Onsen** | Single compact village; *"you don't need a shuttle bus"* | Walk everywhere |
| **Akakura Onsen (Myoko)** | Single walkable onsen village; accommodation and dining together, adjacent to the slopes | Walk everywhere |
| **Hakuba Valley** | Dispersed — Happo, Echoland, Wadano, Misorano, Tsugaike are separate | **Shuttle ends ~6pm; taxi after** |

**This is the strongest micro-location evidence gathered so far, and it cuts against Hakuba** —
the market currently leading the screening on land prices and tourism.

A guest staying in Misorano or Tsugaike who wants to eat in Echoland faces a taxi both ways.
For a lodge whose selling proposition is a week-long stay by four Australians without a car,
that friction is a direct constraint on rate and on repeat visitation.

## 2. Myoko Kogen — Akakura Onsen

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Character | Traditional onsen town; quiet, settled, "old Japan" feel retained. Official sources cite this as matching inbound preference for Japanese culture. |
| Dining | **50+ restaurants and bars** across Akakura Onsen. Japanese, European fusion, Italian, burgers, creperies, sushi, karaage. |
| Nightlife | *"More upbeat than many Japanese ski areas"* but *"low-key bars and post-dinner drinks rather than all-night chaos."* Named venues: Skater Bar, Lover Bar, Kotobuki (karaoke). |
| Convenience | Convenience store in Akakura Onsen. **Supermarket not confirmed in the village.** |
| Onsen | Genuine hot-spring town — the defining feature. |
| Walkability | **Walkable village.** Accommodation, dining and lifts in one place. |
| Car needed | Not for village life. Probably yes to reach Myoko's other 8–9 ski areas. |
| Non-skier week | Plausible — onsen, walkable dining, village atmosphere. Not evidenced. |

**Assessment.** Akakura is structurally well-suited to the owners' model: a self-contained
walkable village with genuine dining depth and an onsen identity, beside the lifts. Combined with
the FIT visitors staying **several weeks to two months** (§ tourism), this is the best *guest
experience* profile of any market examined — and it is the strongest argument for Myoko that does
not depend on the contested appreciation story.

**Gaps:** supermarket presence, medical facilities, pharmacy, childcare, English-language
services, whether restaurants book out in peak weeks, bad-weather options.

## 3. Hakuba Valley

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Character | International, developed, *"more touristy with lots of foreigners."* |
| Accommodation | **Best variety in the region** — luxury through budget. |
| Dining/nightlife | Strong, concentrated in **Echoland**, the main village and **Wadano**. Many venues foreign-owned and operated, English throughout. |
| Walkability | **Within a village yes; across the valley no.** |
| Car needed | **Effectively yes after 6pm**, or taxi budget. |
| Non-skier week | Best-served of the three on breadth of facilities. |

**Assessment.** Hakuba has the deepest infrastructure and the most Australian guests (44.7% of
foreign overnight stays). But it is a **valley of villages, not a village**, and the evening
transport gap means the sub-market a property sits in is decisive.

**This makes the cheap Hakuba fringes (Otari, outer valley) considerably less attractive than
their price suggests** — precisely the areas earlier flagged as a priority for affordability.
Cheap and peripheral in Hakuba means guests are stranded after 6pm.

**Priority sub-markets for a guest-experience-led strategy:** Echoland and the main village.
Misorano (three of six Hakuba candidates) is a **quiet residential area** — good for families,
weaker for the four-snowboarders-for-a-week scenario the brief models.

## 4. Nozawa Onsen

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Character | Traditional working village — a *"thriving, traditional rice farming village"* with festivals through winter and summer. |
| Onsen | *"Probably the winner"* on onsen character; quaint old onsen town feel. |
| Walkability | **Best of all three. No shuttle needed.** |
| Nightlife | Good; named alongside Hakuba as the two decent-nightlife options. |
| Four-season | **Festivals and events in both winter and summer** — a genuine signal, unlike a tourism-board plan. |
| Car needed | **No.** |

**Assessment.** On town structure alone Nozawa is the **strongest of the three**: most walkable,
most authentic, no car required, real year-round village life. It is also **national #2 for land
price appreciation** with a fraction of Myoko's promotional coverage.

The counterweight remains its **565 m base elevation** — lowest of the Honshu candidates, in the
band where Japan Sea side snow decline is evidenced. **Nozawa has the best town and the worst
snow-risk profile of the three.** That tension is genuine and unresolved.

## 5. Seven-day guest experience: four Australian snowboarders

`ESTIMATE` — modelled from the evidence above, **not** from field observation.

| | **Myoko / Akakura** | **Hakuba** | **Nozawa Onsen** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival | Shinkansen to Jōetsu-Myōkō + transfer | Bus/transfer from Nagano | Shinkansen to Iiyama + ~25 min bus |
| Property access | Walk within village | Depends heavily on village | Walk |
| Groceries | Convenience store; **supermarket unconfirmed** | Supermarkets in valley, likely drive | In village |
| Daily mountain transport | Walk to Akakura lifts | Shuttle — **fine during ski hours** | Walk |
| Multi-resort days | Car or bus to 8–9 areas | Shuttle across valley | Nozawa mainly; Madarao nearby by car |
| Dining | 50+ venues, walkable | Excellent **but village-dependent** | Good, walkable |
| Nightlife | Low-key bars | Strongest, in Echoland/Wadano | Good |
| **After 6pm** | **Walk** | **Taxi if crossing villages** | **Walk** |
| Bad-weather day | Onsen, village | Most options overall | Onsen, village |
| Rest day | Onsen town | Broadest choice | Onsen (13 free public baths) |
| Friction | Low within village | **Moderate — evening transport** | **Lowest** |
| Would return | Likely, if snow delivers | Likely | Likely |

**Conclusion for a car-free group of four:** **Nozawa ≥ Akakura > Hakuba**, and Hakuba only
falls behind because of evening transport across a dispersed valley — a factor invisible in
price and tourism data.

This inverts the current screening order on the *guest experience* dimension specifically. It
does not overturn the overall ranking, and should not: Hakuba still leads on evidenced demand
growth, appreciation and snow-elevation profile.

## 6. Micro-location: sub-markets defined

| Market | Sub-market | Character | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hakuba | **Echoland** | Nightlife, dining hub | Named as a main dining/nightlife strip |
| Hakuba | **Happo / main village** | Central, lift access, touristy | Best accommodation variety |
| Hakuba | **Wadano** | Nightlife-adjacent, upper village | Named for nightlife |
| Hakuba | **Misorano** | Quiet residential | 3 of 6 Hakuba candidates sit here |
| Hakuba | **Tsugaike** | Ski-in/ski-out, separate from valley centre | 1 candidate |
| Myoko | **Akakura Onsen** | Walkable core, dining, nightlife, lifts | Primary sub-market |
| Myoko | **Shin-Akakura** | Ski-side, quieter | 1 portal candidate |
| Myoko | **Ikenotaira / Sekigawa / Seki Onsen** | Dispersed, car-dependent | 4 candidates |
| Myoko | **Arai-side town centre** (Gakko-cho, Shinonome-cho, Asahi-cho) | **Not a ski location** | Where the cheap portal stock is |
| Nozawa | **Village core** | Walkable, onsen, dining | Whole village functions as one |
| Yuzawa | **Station area / Naeba / Kandatsu / Iwappara** | Apartment towers, dispersed | 152 apartment listings |

**The Myoko split is the sharpest.** The cheap houses on the portal (¥4.8m, ¥8.99m) are in the
Arai-side town centre — **not a ski location at all**. A buyer screening on Myoko's municipal
median would be looking at a town well away from the snow, which is exactly the failure mode
`DECISIONS.md` D-0012 was written to prevent, now confirmed with real listings.
