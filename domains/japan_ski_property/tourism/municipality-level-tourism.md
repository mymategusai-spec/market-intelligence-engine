**Phase:** 8 · **Status:** Hakuba complete and excellent; Myoko partial; others outstanding
**Last updated:** 2026-08-20
**Data:** [`data/cleaned/tourism/`](../../../data/cleaned/tourism/)

## 1. The test the brief demanded

> *Is Niigata's strong foreign-tourism growth actually occurring in Myoko, or elsewhere in the
> prefecture?*

**Answer: largely elsewhere. Myoko is a minority of the prefecture and is growing more slowly
than Hakuba.**

| | Myoko | Hakuba |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foreign overnight stays 2018 | **59,721** | **164,377** |
| Latest | **120,000** (FY2023) | **447,474** (2024) |
| Growth 2018 → latest | **+101%** | **+172%** |
| Share of own prefecture's foreign stays | **~23%** | — |

`CALCULATION` — Niigata recorded ~820,000 foreign overnight stays in 2025 at +55%, implying
roughly 529,000 in 2024. Myoko's 120,000 (FY2023) is therefore on the order of **23% of the
prefecture**. Even allowing for growth between FY2023 and 2024, Myoko is a **minority
contributor** to Niigata's headline growth.

`FACT` — Niigata's ski facilities are concentrated in **Yuzawa, Minamiuonuma and Myoko**, which
together account for ~87% of the prefecture's ski visitors. Yuzawa — the market this project
already identified as a 33-year value trap — sits inside the same prefecture figure that was
being read as evidence *for* Myoko.

**Conclusion:** the earlier reading, that Niigata's +55% strengthened the Myoko early-stage case,
**does not survive municipality-level data**. Prefecture-wide growth was not evidence for Myoko,
exactly as the brief warned. Hakuba — in the *slower-growing* prefecture — grew faster than Myoko
on both absolute and relative measures.

## 2. Hakuba: 19 years of data, and it is remarkable

`FACT` — Hakuba Village publishes foreign overnight stays by nationality and month back to 2006.

| Year | Foreign overnight stays | Australia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2006 | 33,491 | 7,250 |
| 2014 | 77,724 | 43,094 |
| 2018 | 164,377 | 76,003 |
| 2019* | 278,835 | 166,590 |
| 2021 | 2,041 | 59 |
| 2023 | 252,481 | 92,303 |
| **2024** | **447,474** | **199,885** |

\* 2019–2020 are **mobile-phone-derived estimates**, not accommodation returns — the source says
so explicitly. They are not comparable with the rest of the series and are excluded from growth
calculations below.

`CALCULATION` — CAGR 2006→2024: **15.5%**. CAGR 2014→2024: **19.1%**. Against 2018, the last
methodologically comparable pre-COVID year, 2024 is **+172%**.

### Australians are 44.7% of Hakuba's foreign demand

`FACT` — 2024 by origin: **Australia 199,885 (44.7%)**, Taiwan 36,009 (8.0%), Hong Kong 26,629
(6.0%), USA 23,414 (5.2%), Thailand 23,454 (5.2%), Singapore 22,585 (5.0%).

Two things follow that matter to these owners specifically:

1. **They would be building for their own market.** Australians are the single largest foreign
   group in Hakuba by a wide margin, and grew **+117%** in 2024 alone. Understanding what
   Australian guests want is an advantage these owners actually have.
2. **Hakuba's growth is diversifying, which reduces risk.** Thailand +282%, Taiwan +85%,
   Singapore +47%, Europe +136%. A market dependent on one source country is fragile; Hakuba is
   becoming less so.

### Seasonality is extreme and must not be glossed

`FACT` — Dec–Mar accounts for **84.6%** of all foreign overnight stays in Hakuba. For Australians
specifically it is **97.7%** (Dec 84,537 · Jan 45,680 · Feb 42,902 · Mar 22,189; May–Sep
combined ~2,200).

This is a hard number against the four-season thesis. Australian demand in Hakuba is
**essentially a four-month business**. Any model assuming meaningful Australian summer revenue
is contradicted by measurement.

Note the domestic market is *not* covered by this dataset and is a genuine gap — Nagano
prefecture-wide, Jul–Sep is the largest quarter for total visitors, so summer demand exists; it
is simply not foreign.

## 3. Myoko: smaller, later, and far more concentrated

`FACT` (MLIT Hokushinetsu Transport Bureau, data from Myoko Tourism Management):

- 2017: 45,977 → 2018: **59,721** foreign overnight stays
- **~80%** occur January–March
- Jan–Mar Australian stays ~30,000 of ~46,000 total inbound
- **Australians are ~70% of Myoko's inbound** (a Nikkei report citing a prefecture survey gives 69%)
- Australians are overwhelmingly **FIT**, not package tours
- **Australian visitors reportedly stay from several weeks up to two months**

### The concentration risk nobody mentions

**Myoko is ~70% dependent on a single source market. Hakuba is 44.7%.**

That is a materially worse risk profile, and it cuts directly against the "Myoko is the
undiscovered alternative" narrative. A market whose inbound economy rests on one country is
exposed to that country's currency, airfares, economy and fashion. The AUD/JPY exposure already
identified in the capital model applies **twice** in Myoko — once to the owners' costs, and again
to their guests' willingness to travel.

`HYPOTHESIS` — If AUD weakened materially against JPY, Myoko would lose demand faster than
Hakuba, while the owners' AUD-denominated returns fell at the same time. These exposures are
correlated, not independent. This is testable against past AUD/JPY drawdowns and should be.

### The countervailing point remains, and it is strong

**Stay length.** Several weeks to two months, FIT, in a walkable onsen town where accommodation
and restaurants sit together beside the slopes. For a self-contained lodge that is a materially
better operating profile than high-volume short stays: fewer turnovers, lower cleaning cost per
night, less marketing dependence, higher effective occupancy per booking.

`OPINION` (official source, but promotional) — Akakura Onsen is characterised as quiet and
traditional, matching inbound preference for Japanese culture, with foreign-owned accommodation
businesses increasing.

**Net:** Myoko's demand is smaller, slower-growing and more concentrated than Hakuba's — but of
higher quality per booking. Those are different arguments and should not be conflated.

## 4. Prefecture context: Nagano is not winter-dominant overall

`FACT` — Nagano 2024 tourism visitors (観光地点等入込客数, all visitors, prefecture-wide):
Q1 14,498k · Q2 15,315k · **Q3 19,957k** · Q4 16,026k. Annual 65,797k, up from 61,141k in 2023
(**+7.6%**). Prefecture visitor headcount 40.0m, **+13.2%**; tourism spend ¥1,004,716m, **+17.5%**.

**Nagano's largest quarter is summer, not winter.** That is prefecture-wide and includes
Matsumoto, Karuizawa and the Kamikochi/alpine areas, so it cannot be read directly onto Hakuba —
but it establishes that four-season demand in Nagano is real and domestic, even though foreign
ski demand is overwhelmingly Dec–Mar.

## 6. What changed as a result of this work

1. **The Niigata +55% argument for Myoko is retired.** It was prefecture-level and Myoko is ~23%
   of that prefecture, growing at half Hakuba's rate.
2. **Hakuba's decoupling is now evidenced, not inferred.** 19 years of data, 15.5% CAGR, +172%
   against the last comparable pre-COVID year.
3. **A new risk is on the register:** Myoko's ~70% single-market dependence, correlated with the
   owners' own FX exposure.
4. **The four-season case is weaker for foreign demand than assumed** — 84.6% of Hakuba's foreign
   stays, and 97.7% of Australian stays, fall in Dec–Mar.
